# Proposal Talk — 12-slide skeleton (10–15 min)

The discipline: **one idea per slide, ~1 minute per slide.** Twelve slides is the ceiling,
not the target. If a slide has more than ~25 words or two ideas, split it or cut it.

| # | Slide | The one job | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | RQ in one sentence + your name + domain | 0:30 |
| 2 | The hook | The concrete problem, with one number | 1:00 |
| 3 | Why it matters | Who is hurt by the gap; the stakes | 1:00 |
| 4 | Prior work (the matrix) | The comparison matrix; point at the empty cell | 1:30 |
| 5 | The gap | One sentence: what no one has tested | 1:00 |
| 6 | Research question | The falsifiable RQ, restated, bold | 0:30 |
| 7 | Hypothesis | H1 + the null; what result would refute you | 1:00 |
| 8 | Method | Diagram: data → model/baseline → metric | 1:30 |
| 9 | Baselines & ablations | What you compare against and why | 1:00 |
| 10 | Validity & integrity | Top 2 threats + mitigations; AI-disclosure plan | 1:00 |
| 11 | Timeline | Weeks 9–16 as a Gantt (8-week online section: the remaining modules); the pilot in Week 10 | 1:00 |
| 12 | Expected contribution | What the field gains if H1 holds — and if it fails | 1:00 |

## Slide-design rules
- No paragraphs. Bullets are ≤ 7 words. The audience reads *or* listens — never both.
- Every figure has a caption that stands alone (a reviewer who only sees the slide gets it).
- Put the RQ on slide 1 AND slide 6. Repetition is a feature; people drift.
- Back-pocket (appendix) slides: full bibliography, extra ablations, the threats table.
  You don't show them — you reach for them when a question demands evidence.

## The Q&A
The talk is 10–12 min; questions are the rest. The hardest question is the gift — it tells
you exactly what a reviewer will write. Answer the question asked. If you don't know, say
"I don't know — here's how I'd find out." That sentence is a researcher's, not a fraud's.
