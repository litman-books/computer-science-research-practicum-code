# Double-Blind Anonymity Checklist

Top CS venues (NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, CVPR via their systems) review double-anonymous:
neither side sees the other's identity until camera-ready. De-anonymizing yourself can
get a paper desk-rejected without review. Run this before you submit a paper FOR review
and before you start reviewing a peer's.

## As an AUTHOR — scrub yourself out
- [ ] No author names / affiliations on the title page (acmart: use the `anonymous`
      option → prints "ANONYMOUS AUTHOR(S)").
- [ ] Self-citations are in the third person. NOT "in our prior work [4] we showed…"
      → "Prior work [4] showed…".
- [ ] Acknowledgements section removed or emptied (it names your advisor / lab / grant).
- [ ] Grant / funding IDs stripped (they identify the institution).
- [ ] Repo / data links anonymized (anonymous.4open.science, or an anonymized OSF view)
      — a raw github.com/yourname link de-anonymizes you.
- [ ] PDF metadata cleared (Author field in the document properties).
- [ ] No identifying file paths, usernames, or hostnames in screenshots / logs.
- [ ] Dataset you released is not named after you/your lab in the text.

## As a REVIEWER — protect confidentiality
- [ ] Do NOT paste the paper or its code into any public LLM (confidentiality breach;
      forbidden by NeurIPS / IEEE / CVPR reviewer policies as of mid-2026).
- [ ] Do NOT try to guess or look up who wrote it.
- [ ] Treat the manuscript as confidential — don't share it, don't reuse its ideas.

> De-anonymization is not always cheating — sometimes it's sloppiness. Either way the
> outcome is the same: the reviewers can't unsee it. Run the list.
