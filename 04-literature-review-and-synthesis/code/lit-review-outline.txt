# Literature Review — Annotated Outline (thematic, not paper-by-paper)

> Structure follows Swales' CARS model: establish territory -> establish niche -> occupy niche.
> One subsection per THEME (a column you read DOWN the matrix), not one per paper.

## 0. Opening frame  (~1 paragraph)   [CARS Move 1: territory]
- The problem space and why it matters now.
- Orient a reader who does NOT know your domain.

## 1. Theme A — <e.g., Benchmarks for code-review agents>   (3-6 sentences, several cites)
- Topic sentence = an IDEA about the field, NOT an author's name.
- Synthesize 3+ papers; compare their methods/datasets/metrics.
- End by naming what this theme leaves OPEN.

## 2. Theme B — <e.g., Human-acceptance metrics>
- (same shape)

## 3. Theme C — <e.g., Retrieval strategies>
- (same shape)

## 4. The gap  (1-2 paragraphs)   [CARS Move 2: niche]
- Pull the threads together: the unaddressed column / empty cell.
- Back EVERY gap claim with a cited absence (matrix cell + empty searches).
- "Nobody has done X" must be footnotable, not a hunch.

## 5. Your foothold  (1 sentence)   [CARS Move 3: occupy]
- One sentence: how your planned work occupies that niche.
- Not the experiment yet (that's Ch.8) — just the target.

---
## Self-check before submitting
- [ ] No topic sentence opens with an author's name
- [ ] Each theme cites SEVERAL papers, compared
- [ ] Gap backed by cited absences
- [ ] A stranger could finish the review knowing exactly what you'll do
- [ ] NOTE: this review is a SECTION of your paper, not an arXiv survey
      (arXiv-CS, Oct 31 2025: surveys need prior peer-review acceptance + DOI)
