## The claim, both sides

Write H1 and H0 together. If you cannot write a clean H0, your H1 is not a hypothesis yet (§6.2).

```
H1 (your claim):  [METHOD] achieves [DIRECTION: higher/lower] [PRIMARY DV]
                  than [BASELINE] on [DATASET / SPLIT], over [N >= 5] seeds.

H0 (the null):    [METHOD] produces no difference in [PRIMARY DV]
                  vs. [BASELINE] on [DATASET / SPLIT].

Predicted direction:  __________   Predicted rough magnitude:  __________
```

## Hedge-word blocklist (delete on sight)

These words remove a way for your claim to lose. The Hypothesis & Validity Builder flags them; so do reviewers.

    can        may        might      could      sometimes
    tends to   generally  often      under certain conditions
    help with  improve    (without "than what, by what measure")

## Falsifiability self-check

- [ ] There is a concrete result that would make me say "H1 was wrong."
- [ ] The claim names a comparison (A vs. B), not a lone capability.
- [ ] The claim names a metric, not a vibe.
- [ ] H0 is stated and is genuinely losable.
- [ ] No hedge words survive.

If any box is unchecked, you are still on a higher rung. Climb down.
